\labheader

\begin{goalbox}
Build a defensible cyber risk register for a fictional gas compressor station and use it to argue for three concrete investments.
\end{goalbox}

\begin{stepbox}{1 -- Define your scales}
Define a 5-point \textbf{Likelihood} scale and a 5-point \textbf{Consequence} scale tailored to a midstream gas operator. Consequence dimensions must include \emph{safety, environment, production loss, regulatory}.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{2 -- Brainstorm risks}
List \textbf{ten} cyber risks affecting the station. Examples: ransomware on the Historian, vendor remote-access compromise, USB-borne malware on engineering laptops, SIS bypass via TriStation analogue, etc. Place each in your matrix.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{3 -- Pick top three}
Sort by combined score. For the top three risks, write one Cyber-HAZOP-style row: \emph{guide word, deviation, cyber cause, existing safeguards, gap.}
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{4 -- Investment case}
For each top-three risk, propose one mitigation with rough cost (low / medium / high) and time (this quarter / this year / multi-year).
\end{stepbox}

\begin{deliverbox}
Hand in: the matrix with all ten risks plotted, three Cyber-HAZOP rows, and a 5-line ``CFO summary'' arguing for the three investments.
\end{deliverbox}

\begin{warnbox}
This lab is intentionally fictional. Do not use real plant data, even from public sources -- some incidents are still active.
\end{warnbox}
