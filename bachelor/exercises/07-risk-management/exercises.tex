\noindent\textbf{Goal.} Practise the risk-management toolkit from Section~07 on small but
realistic OT scenarios. Each task is deliberately scoped so that a single
student can answer it in 3--10 sentences (or one small diagram / table).
Use real frameworks where named: IEC~61511, IEC~61882 (HAZOP),
ISA~TR84.00.09, NIST~SP~800-30~R1, AIChE/CCPS LOPA, INL CCE
(Bochman \& Freeman), and FAIR (Jones \& Freund). Cite figures honestly --
``I~estimated'' is fine, ``the vendor told me'' must say so.

\vspace{0.6em}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.1 -- Build a 5$\times$5 Risk Matrix (Water-Treatment Plant)}
You are the security engineer at a small municipal water-treatment plant
(50{,}000 residents served, one chlorine dosing skid, one SCADA HMI,
two remote pumping stations on cellular routers).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Sketch a 5$\times$5 risk matrix with axes \emph{Likelihood}
        (Very Unlikely $\to$ Very Likely) and \emph{Consequence}
        (Negligible $\to$ Catastrophic). Use a green / yellow / orange / red
        colour band of your choice and write down the policy rule
        (which cells are which colour).
  \item Place the following five risks on the matrix and justify the colour
        assignment for each in 1--2 sentences:
        (a)~ransomware on the historian VM,
        (b)~spoofed Modbus write to the chlorine dosing setpoint,
        (c)~stolen maintenance laptop with cached HMI password,
        (d)~loss of cellular link to the south pumping station for 30~min,
        (e)~vendor remote-support session left open over the weekend.
\end{enumerate}
Reference: NIST~SP~800-30~R1, App.~I (likelihood / impact scales);
IEC~62443-3-2 zones \& conduits for the consequence side.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.2 -- Bow-Tie: Unauthorised Setpoint Modification}
Top event: \emph{``unauthorised modification of the chlorine residual setpoint
on the dosing PLC''} (the value that decides how much chlorine is injected).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item List \textbf{three plausible causes} on the left side of the bow-tie
        (mix at least one cyber and one human-error cause).
  \item List \textbf{three consequences} on the right side, covering
        \emph{at least two} of the OT dimensions (safety, environment,
        production, reputation, regulatory).
  \item Place \textbf{two preventive barriers} on the left and
        \textbf{two mitigating barriers} on the right. For each barrier, name
        whether it is procedural, technical, or physical.
\end{enumerate}
A hand sketch (photo / scan) is acceptable; do not over-engineer the diagram.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.3 -- Cyber-HAZOP on Pump P-101}
Process node: pump \texttt{P-101} feeds reactor \texttt{R-201} with cooling
water. Flow setpoint is written by the BPCS over Modbus/TCP; an alarm is
configured at $\pm 20\%$ deviation; no SIS is installed on this loop.
Walk the five core HAZOP guide words from a \emph{cyber} perspective:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No}~flow -- name one cyber-induced cause and the consequence.
  \item \textbf{More}~flow.
  \item \textbf{Less}~flow.
  \item \textbf{Reverse}~flow.
  \item \textbf{As well as} (an unintended additional substance / signal).
\end{enumerate}
For each, write one sentence on the cyber cause and one sentence on the
consequence to \texttt{R-201}. Cite IEC~61882:2016 for the guide-word set
and ISA~TR84.00.09:2017 for the cyber adaptation.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.4 -- LOPA Calculation}
Hazard scenario: \emph{runaway exotherm in reactor \texttt{R-201} caused by
loss of cooling-water flow}. Initiating-event frequency
$f_i = 0.1/\text{year}$ (one failure of \texttt{P-101} expected every
ten years, taken from the maintenance log).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Decide which of the following layers you would credit as
        \emph{independent protection layers (IPLs)} per CCPS LOPA, and assign
        a realistic PFD to each:
        BPCS flow-control loop, high-temperature alarm with operator
        response, SIS SIL-2 trip, mechanical pressure relief valve.
  \item Compute the residual (mitigated) frequency
        $f_\text{mit} = f_i \cdot \prod \text{PFD}_k$.
  \item State one assumption that, if wrong, invalidates the result
        (think \emph{independence}).
\end{enumerate}
PFD ranges to use: BPCS loop $\approx 10^{-1}$, alarm + operator
$\approx 10^{-1}$, SIS SIL-2 $\approx 10^{-2}$ (SIL-3 $\approx 10^{-3}$),
PSV $\approx 10^{-2}$ (CCPS, AIChE, 2001).
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.5 -- CCE Step 1: Consequence Prioritisation}
You are the new plant manager of a mid-sized speciality-chemicals site
(batch reactors, two storage tanks of a toxic intermediate, one rail-loading
spur, $\sim$~400~staff). Following INL's Consequence-Driven Cyber-Informed
Engineering (CCE) methodology, perform \emph{Phase~1} only.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Name the \textbf{top three} ``unacceptable'' outcomes the business
        cannot survive (think existential, not annoying).
  \item Rank them \textbf{1, 2, 3} and explain the ranking rationale in 2--3
        sentences (criteria: loss of life, regulatory licence to operate,
        irreversible environmental damage, weeks of lost production, etc.).
  \item Note which CCE \emph{Phase} comes next and what it produces.
\end{enumerate}
Reference: A.~Bochman \& S.~Freeman, \emph{Countering Cyber Sabotage},
INL/CRC Press, 2021.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.6 -- Risk Response: When Each Choice Is Wrong}
For each of the four ISO~31000:2018 risk responses, give:
\begin{itemize}
  \item one concrete OT example where the response is the
        \emph{right} choice, and
  \item one situation where the same response would be the
        \emph{wrong} choice (and what should be done instead).
\end{itemize}
The four responses: \textbf{Transfer}, \textbf{Accept}, \textbf{Mitigate},
\textbf{Avoid}. Keep each bullet to 1--2 sentences; the trap is treating
``Accept'' as a default or ``Transfer'' as risk reduction.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.7 -- Cyber Insurance and the War Exclusion}
Read a short summary of \emph{Merck \& Co.\ v.\ Ace American Insurance Co.}
(Superior Court of New Jersey, Appellate Division, 2022) -- the case
arose from the 2017 NotPetya wiper that caused USD~1.4~bn of damage at Merck.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item In your own words (2--3 sentences) summarise why the insurer
        invoked the ``hostile or warlike action'' exclusion, and why
        the court held that the exclusion did \emph{not} apply.
  \item State two practical consequences for an OT operator buying
        cyber insurance in 2026 (think: policy wording, sub-limits,
        attribution clauses).
  \item Why is ``insurance'' \emph{not} a substitute for mitigation when
        the consequence dimension is safety?
\end{enumerate}
Reference: Merck v.\ Ace American, A-1879-21, NJ App.\ Div., 2022.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.8 -- Quantitative FAIR Estimate}
Scenario: ransomware encrypts the plant historian (Wonderware / AVEVA),
forcing the operations team to fall back to paper logs for several days
while the VM is rebuilt from offline backup. No production stop, but
regulatory reporting is delayed and a third-party forensic team is engaged.
Compute a FAIR-style \emph{loss exposure} (per Jones \& Freund, 2014):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Estimate the \textbf{Threat Event Frequency (TEF)} per year
        (how often does a ransomware actor reach the historian VM?).
  \item Estimate the \textbf{Vulnerability} (probability that the action
        succeeds given current controls -- EDR, segmentation, backups).
  \item Estimate \textbf{Loss Magnitude (LM)} in EUR -- primary
        (response, recovery, lost productivity) plus secondary
        (regulatory, reputational).
  \item Compute
        $\text{Loss Exposure} = \text{TEF} \times \text{Vuln} \times \text{LM}$
        and show the arithmetic. State the single biggest source of
        uncertainty in your estimate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.9 -- Risk Register Row}
Write \textbf{one complete row} of a risk register for a real risk in the
university lab (or the sample plant used in your project group). Use the
following columns and fill every cell:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ID} (e.g.\ \texttt{R-OT-014})
  \item \textbf{Description} (1 sentence; threat $\to$ vulnerability $\to$ impact)
  \item \textbf{Likelihood} (qualitative, 5-point) and \textbf{Consequence}
        (qualitative, 5-point) with a one-line rationale
  \item \textbf{Owner} (named role, not ``IT'')
  \item \textbf{Treatment} (avoid / mitigate / transfer / accept + control)
  \item \textbf{Residual rating} after treatment
  \item \textbf{Review date} (a real date in 2026 or 2027)
\end{itemize}
Format as a small table or a labelled list. Avoid pseudo-precision
(no ``risk score 7.42''); see Exercise~7.10.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 7.10 -- Spot the False Precision}
A vendor demo tool reports a \textbf{``risk score of 8.42 / 10''} for one of
your PLCs and offers to ``reduce it to 4.10'' if you buy their hardening
package.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Name three things that are methodologically wrong with a
        two-decimal score on a 10-point qualitative scale.
  \item List four questions you would ask the vendor before quoting
        that number to your management (think: input data, weighting,
        independence of factors, calibration).
  \item Suggest one defensible alternative the vendor could offer instead
        (hint: NIST~SP~800-30 semi-quantitative bands, or a FAIR range
        with a stated confidence interval).
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}
