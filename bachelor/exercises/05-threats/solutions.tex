\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 5.2 -- Shodan observations}
Typical findings: hundreds of Modbus endpoints (often building automation), bare Siemens PLCs without firewalls (residential PV inverters, water utilities), Unitronics PLCs with default port and password. Asset owners must remove direct Internet exposure, replace defaults, and use VPN/jump hosts. CERTs/ISPs can notify owners via WHOIS or sectoral channels (e.g.\ Shadowserver).
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 5.3 -- Threat Modelling (sample)}
Ranking for a regional speciality-chemicals site (most $\to$ least likely):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Cybercrime / ransomware (high financial motive, low cost).
  \item Insider (former contractor).
  \item Hacktivist (industry visibility).
  \item Competitor / industrial espionage.
  \item Nation-state APT (only if of strategic interest).
\end{enumerate}
Sample ransomware path: phishing $\to$ corporate AD $\to$ vendor jump host $\to$ engineering WS $\to$ lateral to Historian $\to$ encryption. Choke points: MFA, EDR, segmentation between iDMZ and L2/3, offline backups.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 5.4 -- Supply Chain Response}
Compensating controls: block ingress to vulnerable service on the firewall, deploy a virtual patch on the IPS, disable the vulnerable feature where possible, enable enhanced logging.\\
Inform: plant manager, OT/IT security leads, process safety, vendor account team, legal (in case of regulatory disclosure).\\
Executive briefing: explain in business terms -- ``a critical flaw affects N PLCs; we have temporary controls in place that reduce, but do not eliminate, the risk. Vendor patch in $Q\ldots$, plant outage required.''
\end{taskbox}
