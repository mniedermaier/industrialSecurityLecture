\section*{Goal of this sheet}
Practise placing systems in the IT/OT spectrum, articulating why industrial security is a distinct discipline, and exploring one historical incident in depth.

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.1 -- Identify the System}
For each scenario, decide whether the system is primarily \textbf{IT}, \textbf{OT}, or a \textbf{hybrid (IIoT)}. Justify your answer in 2--3 sentences each.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A SQL database storing customer billing records.
  \item A wind-turbine pitch controller that streams telemetry over MQTT to a cloud broker.
  \item A traffic-light controller at a busy intersection.
  \item A workstation running engineering software used to program PLCs.
  \item A vibration sensor on a pump that publishes to a corporate Kafka topic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.2 -- CIA Re-ordered}
The OT priority order is often written as $A \succ I \succ C$ rather than $C \succ I \succ A$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Give a concrete plant scenario where putting confidentiality first would harm safety.
  \item Give a scenario where strong confidentiality is nonetheless important in OT (think trade secrets, recipes, formulations).
  \item Sketch a short policy statement (3--5 lines) that an asset owner could put in writing to make the priority explicit to all parties.
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.3 -- Group Discussion}
In groups of four:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pick one critical-infrastructure sector (electricity, water, transport, food, healthcare).
  \item List five physical processes within it that depend on digital control.
  \item For each process, name the worst-case outcome of an availability incident.
  \item Prepare a 3-minute pitch summarising your top concern.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{hintbox}
Useful starting points: pumps and SCADA in water utilities, breakers and protection relays in substations, signalling in rail, sterilisation cycles in pharma.
\end{hintbox}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.4 -- Research Mini-Project}
Pick one historical incident and write a 1-page summary (about 400 words):
\begin{itemize}
  \item Maroochy Shire sewage (2000)
  \item Stuxnet (2010)
  \item Ukraine power grid -- BlackEnergy/Industroyer (2015 \& 2016)
  \item TRITON / TRISIS (2017)
  \item Norsk Hydro ransomware (2019)
  \item Colonial Pipeline (2021)
  \item Oldsmar water treatment (2021)
  \item CyberAv3ngers / Unitronics (2023)
\end{itemize}
Your summary must cover: \emph{attacker, target, initial vector, technical steps, physical impact, defensive lessons.}
Cite at least two sources (CISA advisory, vendor post-mortem, peer-reviewed paper).

\begin{warnbox}
Do not interact with or scan any real infrastructure. Use only published material.
\end{warnbox}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.5 -- Cost of an Incident}
For one of the four cost case studies in the slides (Colonial Pipeline, Norsk Hydro, Maersk/NotPetya, Stuxnet), find two public sources and reconstruct:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The headline financial figure -- what is included (ransom, recovery, business interruption)?
  \item One indirect cost often missed (regulatory fines, customer churn, insurance premium increase).
  \item One non-financial cost (reputation, employee morale, safety culture).
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 1.6 -- Industry 4.0 Risk Tour}
For your institution (or a fictional plant) list three Industry 4.0 / IIoT integrations on the roadmap (cloud analytics, digital twin, edge AI, mobile workforce). For each:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Identify one new attack vector it introduces.
  \item Propose one compensating control.
\end{itemize}
\end{taskbox}
