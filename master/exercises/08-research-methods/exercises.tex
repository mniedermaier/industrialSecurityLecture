\noindent
\textit{Goal of this sheet:} build the research-skills muscle a Master thesis or a
peer-reviewed OT-security paper demands. You already own the ICS background; here
you turn that into \emph{defensible} research. Each task asks you to apply a named
method or standard --- the FINER criteria, threats-to-validity taxonomy (Cook \&
Campbell), the ACM Artifact Review badges, ISO/IEC 29147/30111 disclosure, the
Menlo Report --- to a concrete OT situation. The lab (Section~08) walks the same
arc hands-on; this sheet drills the reasoning on paper. Three to ten sentences (or
one tight table) per task. Show the trade-off, not the vocabulary.

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.1 -- From Vague Topic to Falsifiable RQ}
You are handed the topic \emph{``Modbus is insecure --- do something about it.''}
Turn it into research. Produce, in order: (a) one \textbf{research question} that
is specific, measurable, and tractable on the Bachelor lab stack in one session;
(b) one \textbf{falsifiable hypothesis} H1 and its null H0; (c) the explicit
\textbf{scope} (one line in, one line out). Then justify the RQ against all five
\textbf{FINER} criteria (Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant), one
sentence each. A hypothesis you cannot imagine being wrong does not count.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.2 -- Critique an Abstract for Threats to Validity}
Read this verbatim abstract and find what it misses:

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
``We show that our deep-learning IDS detects Modbus injection attacks with
99.7\,\% accuracy. We trained and tested on traffic captured from our SWaT
testbed over one afternoon. Our model outperforms prior work and is ready for
deployment in real plants.''
\end{quote}

Name at least one \textbf{internal}, one \textbf{external}, and one
\textbf{construct} threat to validity this abstract ignores, plus the one
\textbf{statistical/conclusion} red flag in the headline number. For each, say in
one sentence what the authors should have reported instead. (Hint: ask what a
99.7\,\% accuracy hides on an imbalanced dataset.)
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.3 -- Design a Reproducible Experiment}
Design --- on paper, before touching the stack --- a controlled experiment to
answer \emph{``Does increasing the Modbus write-coil injection rate increase the
rate at which a Zeek-based monitor flags unsolicited writes?''} on the Bachelor
Docker stack. Present a single design table with rows for: independent
variable(s) and levels, dependent variable(s) + unit, controlled variables,
baseline / control condition, metric definition, repetitions and order
(randomisation), and at least two named \textbf{confounds with a mitigation}
each. State what result would let you reject H0.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.4 -- Citation Matrix + Methodology Classification}
Build a citation matrix from the five (lightly fictionalised) titles below. For
each row record: \textbf{method} (case study / measurement / experiment / design
science / theory building), \textbf{evaluation substrate} (real plant / testbed /
public dataset / simulation), whether an \textbf{artefact} is plausibly shipped,
and the \textbf{gap} versus an RQ on \emph{reproducible PLC-injection detection}.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item ``A 47-day Shodan census of Internet-exposed Modbus/TCP endpoints'' (2023).
  \item ``TRITON: anatomy of the first safety-system malware'' (2018).
  \item ``GooseHunter: a design-science GOOSE intrusion-detection rule set'' (2022).
  \item ``Scan-cycle jitter as a side channel for PLC tampering: a controlled study'' (2024).
  \item ``A taxonomy of ICS threat-actor TTPs across 14 campaigns'' (2021).
\end{enumerate}
Then state, in one sentence, where your contribution would sit.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.5 -- Ethics, Disclosure, and Dual-Use Statement}
You have found a way to crash a widely deployed PLC family with a malformed
function-code 90 packet, reproduced on a spare unit you own. Write the
\textbf{research-ethics + coordinated-disclosure + dual-use statement} that would
survive a programme committee and an IRB. Cover, in plain language: scope of harm
(what you touched); the \textbf{beneficence-vs-maleficence} argument (Menlo
Report framing); the \textbf{coordinated-disclosure plan} (who you notify ---
vendor PSIRT, coordinating CERT --- and on what timeline, justifying any
deviation from 90/30); and the \textbf{dual-use} line --- what you publish (the
detection / mitigation) versus what you withhold (the turnkey crasher) and why
the balance is positive. Cite the standards you rely on.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.6 -- Is the Results Claim Sound?}
A draft reports: \emph{``Our IDS is better: it scored mean F1 0.91 vs.\ the
baseline's 0.88 across 5 runs. The difference proves our approach is superior.''}
Critique the \textbf{statistical and effect-size soundness} of this claim. Address
at least four of: sample size and variance (is $n=5$ enough?); the absence of a
reported spread (SD / CI); whether a difference of means was tested for
significance and with what test; effect size vs.\ statistical significance; the
multiple-comparisons / cherry-picked-metric risk; and why ``proves'' is the wrong
word. State what you would require before believing the claim.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.7 -- Outline a Reproducibility Package}
Outline the contents of a reproducibility package for the Exercise~8.3 experiment
that targets the ACM \textbf{Artifacts Available + Evaluated (Reusable)} badges.
List the directories/files and, for each, its one-line purpose. Then name the
\textbf{three things that most often break ICS reproducibility} (drawn from the
lecture) and, for each, the concrete pinning step in your package that defeats it.
Finish with the one-command path a stranger runs from clone to figure.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.8 -- Essay: Responsible-Disclosure Timelines for ICS}
Write a structured argument (roughly 250--350 words) on the question: \emph{``Is
Google Project Zero's 90+30-day disclosure clock appropriate for ICS
vulnerabilities, or should ICS use a longer window?''} Take a defensible position.
You must engage with: why ICS patch rollouts differ from IT (maintenance windows,
recertification, fleet heterogeneity, legacy unpatchable assets); the cost of an
\emph{over}-long embargo (users left exposed, no pressure on a slow vendor); the
role of ISO/IEC 29147/30111 and a coordinating CERT (CISA/BSI/JPCERT); and what
you would do when a vendor goes silent. Cite at least two of the standards/policies
from the lecture.
\end{taskbox}

\begin{taskbox}{Exercise 8.9 -- Method Selection (Map Question to Method)}
For each of the four OT-security questions below, name the single best-fit research
method (case study / measurement / experiment / design science / theory building),
justify the choice in two sentences, and name the chief threat to validity that
method invites here.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item ``How many IEC~104 endpoints in Europe answer an unauthenticated read?''
  \item ``Why did the Maroochy Shire sewage attack succeed despite alarms?''
  \item ``Does adding a per-message MAC to GOOSE add unacceptable latency?''
  \item ``Can we build a fuzzer that finds parsing bugs in S7comm stacks?''
\end{enumerate}
\end{taskbox}
