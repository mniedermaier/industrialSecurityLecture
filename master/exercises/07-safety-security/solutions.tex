\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 7.2 -- $\mathrm{PFD}_{avg}$ and SIL band.}
Work in hours: $\lambda_{DU,PT}=3.0\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$,
$\lambda_{DU,FE}=6.0\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$, $T_I=8760\ \mathrm{h}$.
\emph{Sensors (1oo2, $\beta=0.10$):} $\lambda_{DU}T_I=2.628\times10^{-3}$, so
\[
\mathrm{PFD}_S=\tfrac{1}{3}(2.628\times10^{-3})^2
   + 0.10\times 3.0\times10^{-7}\times\tfrac{8760}{2}
   =2.30\times10^{-6}+1.314\times10^{-4}=1.337\times10^{-4},
\]
i.e.\ the $\beta$ common-cause term dominates and the redundancy term is
negligible. \emph{Logic solver (TMR, certified):}
$\mathrm{PFD}_L=2.0\times10^{-4}$. \emph{Final element (1oo1):}
$\mathrm{PFD}_{FE}=\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{DU}T_I=2.628\times10^{-3}$.
\emph{Sum:}
$\mathrm{PFD}_{avg}^{SIF}=1.337\times10^{-4}+2.0\times10^{-4}
+2.628\times10^{-3}=2.96\times10^{-3}$, so
$\mathrm{RRF}=1/2.96\times10^{-3}\approx 338$.

\smallskip
$10^{-3}\le 2.96\times10^{-3}<10^{-2}$ $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{SIL\,2 verified}
(RRF in band $100$--$1000$). \textbf{Dominant term:} the 1oo1 final element
($2.63\times10^{-3}$, $\sim$89\% of the budget). To reach SIL\,3 ($<10^{-3}$)
the cheapest move is to make the final element \textbf{1oo2} (or halve $T_I$ to
$\sim$6 months); halving $T_I$ alone gives $\approx1.3\times10^{-3}$ -- still
SIL\,2, so the valve redundancy is the real lever.
\textbf{Rubric:} correct unit handling (FIT $\to\mathrm{h^{-1}}$), correct
$\beta$ term, the FE identified as dominant, SIL band stated. Common mistake:
forgetting the $\beta$ term so the sensor pair looks falsely good.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{hintbox}
For 1oo2, the $\tfrac{1}{3}(\lambda_{DU}T_I)^2$ term is second-order and
usually swamped by the $\beta\,\lambda_{DU}\,T_I/2$ common-cause term. If your
sensor pair looks two orders better than a single transmitter, you forgot
$\beta$.
\end{hintbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 7.3 -- LOPA independence defeat.}
\emph{Clean case.} Three credited layers:
$\mathrm{PFD}_{SIS}=10^{-2}$, $\mathrm{PFD}_{BPCS\text{-}alarm}=10^{-1}$,
$\mathrm{PFD}_{PSV}=10^{-2}$.
\[
f_{mit}=0.1\times10^{-2}\times10^{-1}\times10^{-2}=1\times10^{-6}/\mathrm{yr}.
\]
\emph{After compromise.} From the SIS-EWS foothold the adversary (i) defeats
\textbf{SIF-1} directly (unsigned logic download, key in PROGRAM) and (ii)
defeats the \textbf{BPCS alarm} too -- the flat OT LAN and shared engineering
path reach the BPCS controller from the same foothold, so the alarm setpoint /
display can be suppressed. These two layers are \emph{no longer independent}:
one compromise removes both. The \textbf{mechanical relief valve} is the only
layer outside the cyber blast radius (no logic, no network).
Set the two cyber-reachable layers to $\mathrm{PFD}=1$:
\[
f_{mit}'=0.1\times 1\times 1\times10^{-2}=1\times10^{-3}/\mathrm{yr}.
\]
\textbf{Loss of protection: three orders of magnitude}
($10^{-6}\to10^{-3}$). The plant now leans entirely on a single mechanical
device. \textbf{Why it matters:} the SIL\,2 certificate is still on the wall
and the malware never had to ``work'' -- the finding is that LOPA's
independence axiom is false under a common cyber cause, exactly the assumption
IEC~61511 \S8 requires and that a shared EWS / flat LAN violates.
\textbf{Rubric:} correct clean and post-compromise arithmetic, identification
of SIS+BPCS-alarm as the now-dependent pair, recognition that the PSV survives,
and the framing that the collapse (not the payload) is the deliverable.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 7.4 -- TRITON/TRISIS kill chain and safe state.}
\emph{Stage map + owned control.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Foothold $\to$ SIS-EWS:} dual-homed NIC bridges DMZ to safety
        net. \emph{Control:} remove the dual-homing -- one-way historian feed
        via data diode; zone the SIS per IEC~62443-3-2.
  \item \textbf{EWS $\to$ controller:} unauthenticated proprietary protocol +
        key-switch in PROGRAM let logic be appended. \emph{Control:}
        key-switch in RUN under dual control (alarm on PROGRAM state); signed /
        whitelisted downloads.
  \item \textbf{Payload:} backdoor appended to the safety application to let
        unsafe states pass while the SIS \emph{appears} healthy.
        \emph{Control:} independent code-integrity / checksum monitoring of the
        running safety application (passive, never inline).
\end{itemize}
\emph{Luck or design?} \textbf{Both, but mostly luck.} TMR fail-safe voting is
\emph{designed} to trip on a detected fault, and the memory-validation mismatch
was caught by that design -- so the safe-state \emph{mechanism} was sound. But
the attackers \emph{intended} a quiet, persistent backdoor; the trip was an
unintended bug in \emph{their} code, not a control that detected the intrusion.
A more careful adversary would have produced a degraded-but-running SIS.
\emph{What TMR buys / doesn't buy:} it buys fail-safe behaviour on
\emph{detected} faults and tolerance of \emph{random} hardware failure; it does
\emph{not} buy protection against authorised-looking logic that voting cannot
distinguish from legitimate code. \emph{Safe-state implication:} after a
successful (bug-free) payload that suppresses the trip output, the SIS will
\emph{not} reach its safe state on a real demand -- the protection is silently
gone. Hence independent integrity monitoring + signed logic are the controls a
regulator will require.
\textbf{Rubric:} per-stage control named; the ``trip was an adversary bug, not
a defence'' nuance; explicit statement that a clean payload defeats the safe
state.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 7.5 -- Non-interfering controls (and one rejected).}
\emph{Accepted -- example 1: signed / whitelisted logic downloads.}
(a) Removes the unsigned-download / appended-logic threat (TRITON path).
(b) Verification is performed on the EWS \emph{at engineering time}, outside
the SIS demand path; it adds no runtime latency, cannot raise
$\mathrm{PFD}_{avg}$, and cannot command the safety output, so no spurious-trip
path is created. (c) Changing the download mechanism is a \emph{security}
change but does not alter the certified safety logic -- so it is an MOC, but
typically \emph{not} a full SIF re-validation if the executed application is
byte-identical.
\emph{Accepted -- example 2: passive code-integrity monitor on a SPAN/tap.}
(a) Detects silent logic tampering / spoofed-healthy status. (b) Read-only on a
mirror port: it has no write path to the logic solver and physically cannot
delay or drop a safety packet, so it is outside the process safety time budget.
(c) No re-validation -- it does not touch the SIF.
\emph{Rejected -- inline IPS / DPI on the SIS conduit.} It sits \emph{inside}
the process safety time: it can buffer, delay, or drop a safety I/O packet, and
a false-positive rule could block a legitimate trip command or force a
spurious trip. Either outcome degrades the safety function, so it is wrong
\emph{by definition}; use passive monitoring instead. (Host AV on the logic
solver is rejected for the same reason -- scan-induced CPU load can eat the
scan-cycle budget.)
\textbf{Rubric:} each accepted control proves non-interference on all three
counts (PFD, latency-in-PST, spurious trip) and states the re-validation flag;
the rejected control names the \emph{specific} interference mechanism, not just
``it's risky''.
\end{solutionbox}

\textbf{Exercise 7.6 -- IEC 61511 $\leftrightarrow$ IEC 62443 lifecycle map.}
\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\noindent\par\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.0cm}p{8.5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{IEC 61511 safety phase} & \textbf{IEC 62443 security activity} \\
\midrule
HAZID / HAZOP & Zone \& conduit partitioning + risk assessment (62443-3-2,
  ZCR steps); joint Cyber-HAZOP \\
SIF allocation (SIL targets) & SL-T allocation per zone/conduit \\
Safety requirements spec (SRS) & Security requirements spec (CRs/SRs of
  62443-3-3) \\
Design \& realisation & Secure-by-design; component SRs (62443-4-2); secure
  development (62443-4-1) \\
Verification \& validation & Security V\&V / FAT-SAT security testing; pen-test
  of the zone \\
Operation \& MOC & Patch \& vulnerability management; security MOC; incident
  response \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\smallskip
\textbf{Shared change gate.} The decisive point is \emph{operation/MOC}: any
edit to the safety application is \emph{simultaneously} a safety MOC (IEC~61511
\S17, may trigger re-validation) \emph{and} a security change (62443 patch/MOC).
A single \textbf{combined change board} (safety lead + security architect +
operations) must gate both; neither process may proceed without the other's
sign-off, because a security patch can invalidate the SIL certificate and a
safety logic edit can open a new attack surface. Two separate boards guarantee a
change slips through one blind spot.
\textbf{Rubric:} correct phase-to-activity pairing (allocation$\to$SL-T,
SRS$\to$CR/SR, V\&V$\to$security V\&V) and an explicit single change-board gate
at MOC with the dual-sign-off rationale.
\end{solutionbox}

\textbf{Exercise 7.7 -- GSN fragment.}
Reference goal tree (G goal, S strategy, C context, Sn solution):
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{G1} HF-201 SIF-1 achieves SIL\,2 \emph{and} is not defeatable
        by a credible cyber adversary.
  \item \quad\textbf{C1} Threat model (TRISIS-class, SIS-EWS foothold);
        \textbf{C2} proof-test regime $T_I=1\,$yr.
  \item \quad\textbf{S1} Argue over the two orthogonal properties: integrity
        \emph{and} non-defeatability.
  \item \qquad\textbf{G2} $\mathrm{PFD}_{avg}<10^{-2}$ demonstrated.
        $\to$ \textbf{Sn1} Ex.\,7.2 calculation ($2.96\times10^{-3}$).
  \item \qquad\textbf{G3} Safety-function integrity preserved vs.\ threat model.
  \item \qquad\quad\textbf{G3.1} Tamper path closed $\to$ \textbf{Sn2}
        Ex.\,7.5 controls (signed logic, zoning, key custody).
  \item \qquad\quad\textbf{G3.2} Tampering detectable $\to$ \textbf{Sn3}
        passive integrity monitor evidence.
  \item \qquad\quad\textbf{G3.3} Change cannot bypass assurance $\to$
        \textbf{Sn4} Ex.\,7.6 shared change-board gate.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Why load-bearing:} G1 is \emph{false} unless G3 holds, so the security
argument is a child of the top safety goal, not a side annex. A SIL certificate
that argues only G2 is an \emph{incomplete} case -- it asserts integrity under
the random-fault model while ignoring the systematic common-cause an adversary
introduces.
\textbf{Rubric:} two sub-goals under one strategy, evidence nodes traced to the
right exercises, and the explicit statement that G3 conditions G1. (Indent
depth above just denotes tree level; a TikZ tree is equally acceptable.)
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 7.8 -- Essay (model points).}
A first-class answer makes three moves.
\textbf{(i) The maxim is sound as a constraint, not a slogan.} A security
control that can command, delay, or spuriously trip the safety output has been
\emph{added to the safety function's failure space}: it can raise
$\mathrm{PFD}_{avg}$ or create a new spurious-trip path. That is why inline IPS
on the SIS conduit and host AV on the logic solver are rejected -- they are
inside the process safety time. The safe state always wins; the security job is
to stop an adversary \emph{preventing} that state, never to add a new way to
mis-fire it.
\textbf{(ii) The converse failure is real.} ``The certificate forbids change''
is weaponised to leave a reachable SIS unpatched and unzoned. The resolution is
\emph{process}: the IEC~61511 MOC + a shared change board let a security
improvement proceed with the right re-validation, rather than freezing the
plant in a known-vulnerable state. Refusing all change is itself a safety
decision -- and usually the wrong one once the SIS is provably reachable.
\textbf{(iii) Genuine conflict + adjudication.} A vendor recovery tunnel
improves availability/recoverability but enlarges attack surface. Adjudicate by
ALARP-style reasoning: keep the capability but make it brokered, MFA-gated,
recorded, and default-closed -- buying recoverability without a standing
ingress. \textbf{Grounding:} TRITON showed attackers pursue the SIS directly;
IEC~61511 supplies the MOC discipline and IEC~62443-3-2 the zoning; ISA
TR84.00.09 ties them together.
\textbf{Rubric:} all three moves present; the essay must avoid treating the
maxim as forbidding \emph{all} change. Bonus for the ALARP framing of the
genuine-conflict case and for citing $\geq 2$ standards correctly.
\end{solutionbox}
