\noindent
\textbf{Note.} Model answers are concise; open-ended tasks carry a rubric hint stating what must be
present for full marks. Technique IDs follow MITRE \emph{ATT\&CK for ICS}; accept any defensible ID
that sits in the correct tactic and is justified. Mark on reasoning, source-citation, and
safety-awareness, not on exact ID matches.

\begin{hintbox}
Three habits unlock most of this sheet: (1)~think \emph{passive before active, read before write,
paper before live} -- the safe ordering is itself the answer to several tasks; (2)~every offensive
action has both an ATT\&CK ID \emph{and} a detection footprint, and the gap between ``what I did''
and ``what the sensor saw'' is where the report's value lives; (3)~for SIS/safety questions, ask
what the safe state is and what it costs the attacker to defeat it \emph{quietly}.
\end{hintbox}

\begin{warnbox}
\textbf{Authorised use only -- non-negotiable.} Every technique modelled here is lawful only inside
a contained lab you own or a written, scoped, signed engagement. Pointing any of it at a system you
have no ROE for is a crime (\S\,202a/\S\,202c StGB, CFAA, Computer Misuse Act); against a live OT
process it can injure or kill. There is no ``I was only testing'' defence. If a student's answer
treats unauthorised action as acceptable in any scenario, it fails regardless of technical quality.
\end{warnbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.1 -- ROE and scope (model + mark scheme).}\\[2pt]
A pass-grade ROE makes every clause named and testable:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \emph{Scope (in):} SCADA master \texttt{10.0.10.5}, PLC sites \texttt{10.0.20.0/24}, eng.\
        workstation \texttt{10.0.10.40}, historian \texttt{10.0.10.60}.
        \emph{Scope (out, explicit):} the Triconex SIS and its network, corporate AD/IT, anything
        Internet-facing, and any device not listed -- out-of-scope is a \emph{deny-list by default}.
  \item \emph{Timing:} test 09:00--16:00 weekdays only; exclusion windows during peak demand and any
        maintenance; named kill-switch contact reachable 24/7 with a stated max response time.
  \item \emph{Approval chain:} operations manager, CISO, and process-safety engineer each
        counter-sign; the safety engineer's signature attests that the in-scope actions cannot, by
        design, place a demand on the SIS.
  \item \emph{Communications:} start-of-day go/no-go, daily written status, immediate-escalation
        triggers (any unexpected process deviation, any device unresponsive), end-of-engagement
        evidence handover with chain of custody.
  \item \emph{Forbidden actions:} no DoS against safety systems, no writes to production
        controllers, no SIS testing, no pivot outside scope.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Mark scheme:} pass requires named scope (in \emph{and} explicit out), an explicit forbidden
list centred on safety, a 24/7 kill-switch, and an evidence-handover/chain-of-custody clause.
Deduct for vague language (``best-effort'', ``where possible''). \emph{Source:} NIST SP~800-115;
PTES Pre-engagement.
\end{solutionbox}

\textbf{Exercise 4.2 -- ICS Cyber Kill Chain mapping.}\\[2pt]
Example (Stuxnet); other campaigns acceptable.
\par\begin{tabular}{@{}p{7.0cm}p{7.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Stage~1 -- intrusion} & \textbf{Stage~2 -- ICS attack} \\
\midrule
Recon of contractor / supply chain to a target with no Internet path. &
Develop centrifuge-specific logic to drive Vacon/Fararo Paya VFDs out of envelope. \\
Weaponise USB + LNK / print-spooler 0-days; sign driver with stolen cert. &
Test against a mirror cascade (the attacker had a model of the plant). \\
Deliver across the air gap on removable media; install; limited C2. &
Deliver/modify the S7-315 OB via the Step7 DLL hook. \\
Act: spread on the engineering LAN, locate Step7 project. &
Execute: vary rotor speed while replaying 21\,s of recorded telemetry to the HMI. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[3pt]
\emph{Why the split matters:} Stage~1 is generic IT tradecraft and is the cheapest place to break
the chain (patching, segmentation, removable-media control, MFA) before the adversary ever
acquires the ICS-specific capability that Stage~2 requires. \emph{Source:} M.\ Assante \&
R.\ Lee, \emph{The Industrial Control System Cyber Kill Chain}, SANS, 2015; Symantec
\emph{W32.Stuxnet Dossier}, 2011.\\[2pt]
\emph{Rubric hint:} full marks require both stages populated and one sentence on the
defender-leverage point (break Stage~1 cheaply).
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.3 -- Recon that won't crash a fragile PLC.}\\[2pt]
(1)~\emph{Passive first:} ARP/LLDP/CDP listening to enumerate devices and topology; 24\,h passive
Zeek/SPAN capture to learn protocols, polling cadence and the legitimate master(s); read-only HTTP
GET of any web UI banner. These reveal \emph{who talks to whom and how}, but \emph{not} closed ports
or precise firmware -- and they send the target nothing, so they cannot trip it.\\
(2)~\emph{Active, lowest-risk:} a TCP \emph{connect} scan (\texttt{nmap -sT -Pn}) with
\texttt{--max-rate 5 --scan-delay 200ms} on a short, known ICS port list. Connect-scan completes the
handshake and tears down cleanly, which fragile stacks tolerate far better than half-open SYN
floods or UDP storms that leave dangling state. Then a \emph{single}, vendor-safe NSE identity
script (e.g.\ \texttt{modbus-discover}) at one request, observed, before deciding on anything deeper.\\
(3)~\emph{Refuse:} (a)~malformed-packet / fuzzing NSE scripts -- legacy ICS stacks can hang or fault
on out-of-spec frames; (b)~broadcast / mass UDP enumeration -- can saturate a fragile device and
trip a watchdog. A write-based ``identify'' probe is likewise off-limits: any write can change
process state.\\
(4)~\emph{Health check between steps:} confirm the PLC still answers a benign read (e.g.\ a single
FC3 holding-register read) and that the legitimate master's poll cadence in \texttt{conn.log} is
unchanged before escalating. \emph{Source:} CISA recommended practices; NIST SP~800-115 (rate-limit
active testing on fragile targets).
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.4 -- Five offensive actions to ATT\&CK for ICS.}\\[2pt]
\par\begin{tabular}{@{}p{6.4cm}p{3.6cm}p{3.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Action} & \textbf{Technique ID} & \textbf{Tactic} \\
\midrule
Enumerate hosts / open ICS ports & T0846 Remote System Discovery & Discovery \\
Unauthenticated Modbus write coil & T0855 Unauthorized Command Message & Impair Process Control \\
Replay telemetry / false HMI view & T0856 Spoof Reporting Message & Inhibit Response Function \\
Stop the S7 CPU & T0858 \emph{(Change Operating Mode)} \textbar{} T0816 Device Restart/Shutdown & Inhibit Resp.\ / Impair \\
\texttt{RUN}\,$\to$\,\texttt{PROGRAM} to load logic & T0858 Change Operating Mode (then T0843 Program Download) & Inhibit Resp.\ / Lateral / Exec. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[3pt]
\emph{Notes on the ambiguous rows:} a CPU stop can be argued as T0858 (mode change to halt scan) or
T0816 (device shutdown) -- accept either with justification; T0814 Denial of Service is acceptable
if the candidate frames it as availability loss. The replay action is T0856 (spoofed reporting); if
the candidate stresses the operator's blinded view, T0832 Manipulation of View is also defensible.
Loading logic pairs T0858 (enter PROGRAM) with T0843 Program Download.\\[2pt]
\emph{Rubric hint:} mark on correct tactic column + defensible ID; penalise putting protocol-write
sabotage under ``Impact'' when ``Impair Process Control'' is the precise column. \emph{Source:}
MITRE ATT\&CK for ICS.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.5 -- TRITON-style SIS-disable scenario (paper only).}\\[2pt]
(1)~\emph{Two-part attack:} the SIS step must \emph{neutralise the trip} -- alter the safety logic
or set-points so a real demand (e.g.\ over-temperature on the fired heater) no longer trips the
plant to safe state. The separate BPCS step must then \emph{create the dangerous condition} (drive
fuel/temperature past the safe envelope). Only the combination is lethal: the SIS exists precisely
to catch a BPCS excursion, so the attacker must blind the last-resort layer \emph{and} drive the
process.\\
(2)~\emph{Safe-state implication:} on a valid demand the SIS is engineered to fail to a defined safe
state (de-energise to trip, close fuel, shut the unit down). That ``fail-safe'' bias is exactly what
the attacker must defeat \emph{silently} -- a noisy attempt makes the SIS trip, which stops the
process and reveals the intrusion.\\
(3)~\emph{Why TRITON was found:} a flaw in the attacker's payload caused the Triconex to fail-safe
trip, shutting the plant down and triggering an investigation that surfaced the implant. That makes
a \emph{quiet} SIS attack extremely hard: the safe-state design means any mistake announces you, so
the attacker needs near-perfect knowledge of the controller internals.\\
(4)~\emph{ATT\&CK:} T0880 Loss of Safety (and T0858 Change Operating Mode / T0889 Modify Program for
the logic change); T0843 Program Download if logic is reflashed. \emph{Source:} Dragos
\emph{TRISIS} 2017; Mandiant \emph{TRITON} 2017/2018.\\[2pt]
\emph{Rubric hint:} full marks require the SIS/BPCS \emph{layering}, the safe-state property, and
why \emph{quiet} is the hard part.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.6 -- Detection mapping (answer key).}\\[2pt]
\par\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.2cm}p{2.4cm}p{6.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Action} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Why} \\
\midrule
Passive sniff / baseline & Miss & sends nothing; the sensor sees no new flow from you. \\
Low-rate connect scan & Partial & no notice fires, but a burst of short flows from one source is
visible in \texttt{conn.log} to anyone who looks. \\
Read-only FC~1--4 enumeration & Miss & rule fires only on write FCs; reads log to
\texttt{modbus.log} with no notice. \\
Unauthorised \texttt{WRITE\_SINGLE\_COIL} & Detect & FC5 from a non-eng host raises
\texttt{Unsolicited\_Write}. \\
30\,s interlock-defeat write storm & Detect (loud) & continuous FC5 floods \texttt{notice.log} --
the opposite of stealthy. \\
HMI replay using only reads & Miss & all read PDUs; nothing for a write-only rule to match. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[3pt]
\emph{Gap-closing class:} \emph{behavioural / analytics} detection, not signatures. Concrete signals
it would track: per-source write-rate and \emph{new-master} detection (a host that never wrote
suddenly writing), and transaction-ID / payload-replay detection for the read-only replay case --
plus a baseline-deviation alert on the scan's flow burst.\\[2pt]
\emph{Rubric hint:} the read-only enumeration and the read-only replay are the two students most
often get wrong -- both are \emph{missed} by a write-only rule. Full marks require naming
behavioural detection \emph{and} one concrete signal.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.7 -- ``Impact'' section (model).}\\[2pt]
\emph{Model narrative.} Any host on the control VLAN can command the conveyor PLC directly: with no
authentication and no write allow-list, a single unauthenticated Modbus write sets or clears the
motor output, overriding the operator and the control logic. The immediate process consequence is
uncommanded actuator motion -- the conveyor can start or stop on an attacker's timing, independent
of the start/stop and guard interlocks as seen at the protocol layer. The worst credible outcome is
motion while a guard should hold the line stopped (maintenance, a cleared jam), creating an
entrapment or crush hazard; a sustained write storm can hold the output against the interlock for as
long as the attacker is on the wire. The safety dimension is therefore real: this finding can place
a demand on the protective function and, in the worst case, contribute to personal injury, so it is
a safety-relevant finding, not merely an availability one. Likelihood is high \emph{given} VLAN
access -- the action needs only L2 reach and a standard Modbus client, no credentials, no exploit
and no special knowledge beyond the coil address. We rate the process impact \textbf{High /
safety-relevant}: low attacker effort, direct actuator control, and a credible path to physical
harm; the CVSS-E score is secondary to that process-and-safety judgement.\\[2pt]
\emph{Mark scheme:} must cover process consequence + worst credible outcome, the safety dimension
(could it injure people), likelihood tied to the access required, and a process-impact rating that
is \emph{not} just a CVSS number. Including repro steps or recommendations here = deduct (wrong
section).
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}
\textbf{Exercise 4.8 -- Ethics / de-confliction essay (rubric, 5 points).}\\[2pt]
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Authorised vs.\ red-team vs.\ unauthorised} (1\,pt). Distinguishes the three on the
        basis of \emph{signed scope}: a pentest is fully authorised and coordinated; a blind
        red-team is authorised by leadership but the blue team is not told; unauthorised access is a
        crime regardless of intent. ``Verbal permission'' is treated as \emph{no} permission.
  \item \textbf{Necessary-but-not-sufficient} (1\,pt). Argues that owner consent does not cover
        third-party human safety; process-safety engineers and on-shift operators must be in the
        loop, and live-plant offensive testing is defensible only when the test cannot place a demand
        on safety functions (or is restricted to read-only / shadow environments).
  \item \textbf{De-confliction mechanics} (1\,pt). Concrete: pre-agreed source IPs / a known test
        window / out-of-band channel to the SOC so the team is not mistaken for a real adversary, and
        a documented procedure for telling a \emph{real} incident during the window apart from test
        traffic (e.g.\ the team pauses and the SOC verifies against the test log).
  \item \textbf{Personal red lines} (1\,pt). Names specific refusals even with a signed contract --
        e.g.\ no SIS testing on a live unit, no DoS on safety-related networks, no destructive write
        without a process engineer present and a reviewed safety case -- each tied to a principle
        (do-no-harm, reversibility, human safety over deliverables).
  \item \textbf{Writing, structure, citation} (1\,pt). At least one primary source cited (NIST
        SP~800-115, PTES, ISO/IEC~29147/30111, or relevant law); no invented sources; coherent
        argument rather than a checklist.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Rubric hint:} an essay that concludes ``a signed contract makes anything fine'' caps at 2/5 --
the whole point is that human safety can override the contract.
\end{solutionbox}
