\begin{frame}{Bias Awareness}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Vendor PSIRTs under-disclose -- exploits exist for years before CVE.
  \item Sector ISACs over-rotate to flagship members.
  \item Commercial intel companies push their products in the reports.
  \item Government attribution is selective and political.
  \item Underground forum chatter is partly noise and partly disinformation.
\end{itemize}
\begin{warnbox}
Treat any single source as a hypothesis. Confirm a campaign with at least two independent sources before acting on its scope.
\end{warnbox}
\source{Threat-intel methodology: Caltagirone, S. et al., \emph{Diamond Model}, 2013.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Common Failure Modes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item ``Intel'' that nobody reads (no consumer, no follow-up).
  \item IOCs deployed without context, generating alert fatigue.
  \item Detection improvements that never make it back to the platform.
  \item Single-source dependence (one vendor feed).
  \item Confusing CTI with marketing reports.
\end{itemize}
\begin{warnbox}
A CTI team without a tight feedback loop into detection engineering is decoration. Measure the time from \emph{intel published} to \emph{rule deployed}.
\end{warnbox}
\source{SANS ICS \emph{CTI Survey} 2022--2024 \textbar{} Dragos \emph{Year in Review} \textbar{} Mandiant \emph{M-Trends} \textbar{} Bianco, D., \emph{Pyramid of Pain}, 2013.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Framework anchor for this section}
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=2mm, scale=0.85, transform shape]
  \node[isbox, fill=isOT!15, draw=isOT, minimum width=110mm, font=\small] (a)
    {\textbf{ATT\&CK for ICS} \ TTP taxonomy --- 12 tactic columns, technique IDs (T08xx) you map both ways};
  \node[isbox, fill=isPLC!15, draw=isPLC, minimum width=110mm, font=\small, below=of a] (b)
    {\textbf{Diamond Model} \ one intrusion event = adversary / capability / infrastructure / victim + meta-features};
  \node[isbox, fill=isAccent!15, draw=isAccent, minimum width=110mm, font=\small, below=of b] (c)
    {\textbf{STIX 2.1} \ object graph: \texttt{attack-pattern}, \texttt{indicator}, \texttt{malware}, \texttt{intrusion-set}, relationships};
  \node[isbox, fill=isAttack!15, draw=isAttack, minimum width=110mm, font=\small, below=of c] (d)
    {\textbf{TAXII 2.1} \ HTTPS collections + channels --- the transport that moves STIX between peers};
\end{tikzpicture}
\par\vspace{2mm}
{\footnotesize ATT\&CK names the \emph{behaviour}; the Diamond Model frames a single \emph{event}; STIX/TAXII make both \emph{machine-readable and shareable}.}
\source{MITRE, \emph{ATT\&CK for ICS} \textbar{} Caltagirone, Pendergast, Betz, \emph{The Diamond Model}, 2013 \textbar{} OASIS \emph{STIX 2.1} / \emph{TAXII 2.1}, 2021.}
\note{
  Anchor frame: the three frameworks are complementary, not competing. ATT\&CK is the verb list --- when an analyst writes ``T0859 Valid Accounts'' every reader in the field knows exactly what was done. The Diamond Model is the atom of analysis: one event ties four corners together, and pivoting from any one corner (e.g.\ a C2 IP) to the others is the core analytic move. STIX 2.1 is the data model that encodes both --- an \texttt{attack-pattern} object can carry an external reference straight to an ATT\&CK technique ID, and \texttt{relationship} objects wire the Diamond corners together. TAXII is just the pipe. Pre-empt the misconception that STIX = IOC dumps: STIX expresses TTPs, campaigns and intrusion-sets, not only hashes. Transition: ``Let us walk one real campaign around the Diamond.''
}
\end{frame}

Industroyer2 is the cleanest public Stage-1/Stage-2 example: ELECTRUM (Dragos name; capability and infrastructure overlap heavily with Sandworm/GRU Unit 74455) first established an IT foothold and moved laterally (Stage~1 --- generic intrusion tradecraft, mappable to ATT\&CK Enterprise), then pivoted into the OT segment to deploy Industroyer2, a purpose-built binary speaking IEC~60870-5-104 to issue breaker-open commands, paired with CaddyWiper to destroy the recovery path (Stage~2 --- ATT\&CK for ICS, Impact). Emphasise that the Stage-2 capability was hard-coded to the specific substation's configuration --- this was a tailored, not opportunistic, attack. Note CERT-UA + Microsoft assess the attack was contained before the scheduled detonation. Transition: ``ELECTRUM is one of a small named set --- here is the wider landscape.''

%% Frame 4: Consequence-driven intelligence requirements (PIRs)
\begin{frame}{Consequence-driven intelligence requirements}
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=3mm, scale=0.85, transform shape]
  \node[isbox, fill=isSafety!12, draw=isSafety, minimum width=120mm, font=\small] (a)
    {\textbf{Crown-jewel consequence} \ ``loss of the boiler-feed pump trips the unit''};
  \node[isbox, fill=isOT!15, draw=isOT, minimum width=120mm, font=\small, below=of a] (b)
    {\textbf{PIR} \ ``Which groups have demonstrated capability against our SIS / Modbus pumps?''};
  \node[isbox, fill=isAccent!15, draw=isAccent, minimum width=120mm, font=\small, below=of b] (c)
    {\textbf{Collection} \ Dragos/E-ISAC feeds, vendor PSIRT, honeypot telemetry tagged to that consequence};
  \node[isbox, fill=isPLC!15, draw=isPLC, minimum width=120mm, font=\small, below=of c] (d)
    {\textbf{Analysis $\to$ dissemination} \ finished assessment to SOC + plant ops, with a deadline};
\end{tikzpicture}
\par\vspace{1.5mm}
\begin{notebox}{Drive collection from consequence, not from feeds}
A PIR is answerable, time-bound and owned by a decision-maker. TLP governs who sees it: \textbf{RED} named-recipient only, \textbf{AMBER+STRICT} org-internal, \textbf{GREEN} community, \textbf{CLEAR} public. Tag every artefact at ingest --- mislabelled sharing is the program-ending mistake.
\end{notebox}
\source{CISA \emph{Cyber-Informed Engineering} / consequence-driven CCE (INL) \textbar{} FIRST \emph{TLP 2.0} \textbar{} U.S.\ ICD~204 PIR practice.}
\note{
  The closing analytic discipline of the section. Reverse the usual flow: most teams collect feeds and then look for relevance; a mature OT program starts from the crown-jewel consequence (here, an INL/CCE-style ``loss of the boiler-feed pump trips the unit'') and derives Priority Intelligence Requirements from it. A good PIR is answerable, time-bound, and has a named consumer who will \emph{act} on the answer --- contrast with the ``intel nobody reads'' failure mode named later in the deck. Walk the cycle top-to-bottom: consequence $\to$ PIR $\to$ targeted collection (note collection is now scoped, cutting the low-signal STIX volume problem from the existing STIX frame) $\to$ analysis $\to$ dissemination with a deadline. TLP 2.0 handling is non-negotiable in ISAC sharing: RED is named-eyes-only, AMBER+STRICT restricts to the recipient org, GREEN is community-wide, CLEAR is public --- and the fatal mistake is mis-tagging on ingest, which either leaks a source or buries actionable intel. Tie back: this is how the threat-group table (Frame~3) becomes a collection plan rather than trivia. Transition into the existing programme-building frames.
}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Summary -- Section \sectionNumber}
\begin{takeawaybox}{OT threat intelligence}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item Three layers (strategic, operational, tactical) -- a programme delivers all three.
  \item Six source families with known biases -- triangulate, do not trust one.
  \item ATT\&CK for ICS, Diamond Model, Pyramid of Pain are the operating frameworks.
  \item Hunt with hypotheses, not haystacks; feed results back to the SOC.
  \item Measure time from intel publication to deployed detection.
\end{itemize}
\end{takeawaybox}
\end{frame}
