\begin{goalbox}
Lecturer / TA reference: model research question and hypothesis, a worked
citation matrix, a complete experiment design with variables and metrics,
a model ethics + disclosure statement, the common mistakes to watch for,
and a grading rubric. This is a research-skills lab -- reward defensible
reasoning and reproducibility over a particular ``right'' answer.
\end{goalbox}

\begin{solutionbox}{Step 1 -- Model RQ + falsifiable hypothesis}
A sharp, testbed-tractable example (accept any RQ that is specific,
measurable, and runnable on the Bachelor stack in one session):

\vspace{1mm}
\textbf{RQ:} Does increasing the Modbus/TCP write-coil injection rate
change the per-minute detection count of the stack's Zeek
\texttt{Unsolicited\_Write} rule?

\textbf{H1:} A higher injection rate yields a measurably higher
detections/min count (monotonic up to the rule's window saturation).

\textbf{H0:} Injection rate has no measurable effect on detections/min.

\textbf{Scope IN:} one protocol (Modbus/TCP), one component (OpenPLC +
Zeek), one metric (detections/min). \textbf{Scope OUT:} other protocols,
real PLCs, evasion techniques.

\vspace{1mm}
\emph{Grading:} full marks for exactly one RQ, one H1, one H0, all
falsifiable, with an explicit in/out scope. Deduct for multi-part
questions, unmeasurable verbs (``understand'', ``explore''), or a
hypothesis that cannot be wrong.
\end{solutionbox}

A model matrix; the load-bearing column is the rightmost (gap vs.\ the
student's RQ). Accept 8--12 rows with logged search strings.

\toprule
\textbf{Paper (key)} & \textbf{Year} & \textbf{Method} & \textbf{Testbed/data} & \textbf{Artefact} & \textbf{Gap vs.\ RQ} \\
\midrule
\texttt{goh16}    & 2016 & measurement & SWaT             & dataset    & no detection-rate metric \\

\texttt{ids22}    & 2022 & experiment  & SWaT             & code (git) & single rate, no baseline \\
\texttt{fuzz20}   & 2020 & design sci. & OpenPLC          & code       & fuzzing, not detection \\
\texttt{census21} & 2021 & measurement & Shodan           & no         & internet-wide, not testbed \\

\vspace{1mm}
\emph{Grading:} reward correct method classification, presence of an
``artefact?'' column (reproducibility awareness), and a non-empty,
specific gap column. Penalise a matrix that is just a bibliography with
no analytical columns, or a gap column that says ``none'' for every row
(then the RQ is not novel -- they must revisit Step 1). Search strings
and venues (S4, ACSAC, USENIX, IEEE S\&P, ESORICS, WOOT, ICS-CSR) must
be logged.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}{Step 3--5 -- Worked experiment design + analysis}
Model design for the RQ above:

\vspace{1mm}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.0cm}p{11.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Element} & \textbf{Model value} \\
\midrule
Independent variable   & injection rate \{0 (control), 1, 10, 50\} writes/s \\
Dependent variable     & Zeek \texttt{Unsolicited\_Write} detections per minute \\
Controlled variables   & stack commit, container CPU limit, MTU, payload size, run window (60\,s) \\
Baseline / control     & rate 0 = idle poll loop, no injection \\
Metric + unit          & detections/min; secondary: PLC scan-cycle jitter (ms) \\
Repetitions            & 5 runs per condition; report mean + spread \\
Order                  & randomised condition order to remove warm-up bias \\
Confounds + mitigation & noisy-neighbour $\to$ pin CPU; clock drift $\to$ NTP-sync host \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{1mm}
\textbf{Model outcome.} The control (rate 0) yields $\approx$0
detections/min; detections rise with rate until the rule's window
saturates. A defensible write-up rejects H0 with the figure from
\texttt{plot\_results.py} regenerated from \texttt{results/run.csv}.
A ``no significant effect'' result is equally acceptable if the data
support it and the student says so honestly.

\vspace{1mm}
\emph{Grading:} the control condition must be present and run first;
the figure must be script-generated from CSV (not a screenshot); the
H0 decision must follow from the numbers, not from the student's prior.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}{Step 6 -- Model ethics + disclosure + dual-use statement}
A passing \texttt{ethics.md} contains, in plain language:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Scope of harm:} only the local Bachelor testbed touched; no third party, no production plant, no PLC the student does not own.
  \item \textbf{Beneficence vs.\ maleficence (Menlo):} defenders gain a tuned detection metric; the only ``attack'' is write-coil injection already public since the early Modbus literature, so net benefit is positive.
  \item \textbf{Coordinated disclosure:} no vendor to notify on a lab stack -- but the student states what they \emph{would} do for a real finding (vendor PSIRT $\to$ coordinating CERT BSI/CISA, ICS-realistic 180/60 timeline, deviation justified).
  \item \textbf{Dual-use:} publish the detection method and metric; withhold any turnkey exploit; benefit-to-harm argument written out.
  \item \textbf{Respect for persons / data:} no PII; any sensitive data anonymised with justification.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Grading:} all five points present and specific to the study (not a
boilerplate paste) earns full ethics marks. Missing disclosure plan or
missing dual-use reasoning is a hard deduction -- see the rubric.
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{solutionbox}{Common mistakes to flag while grading}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Scope creep:} an RQ that needs more than the testbed and one session -- that is a thesis, not a lab. Tell them to cut to one IV and one DV.
  \item \textbf{No baseline:} a treatment number with no control. Uninterpretable; send back to Step 4.
  \item \textbf{HARKing:} a ``hypothesis'' clearly written after seeing the results. The Step-9 pre-registration commit (git timestamp) is the defence; check the log order.
  \item \textbf{Screenshot science:} a figure pasted from the screen instead of regenerated by a committed script -- not reproducible.
  \item \textbf{Unpinned environment:} no stack commit hash, no \texttt{requirements.txt}; ``works on my machine''. Re-running from a fresh clone exposes it.
  \item \textbf{Citation theatre:} a bibliography with no analytical gap column; cannot support a novelty claim.
  \item \textbf{Ethics as afterthought:} disclosure / dual-use bolted on at the end rather than written before findings.
\end{itemize}
\end{solutionbox}

\begin{rubricbox}
Total 100 pts; pass mark 50.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Question clarity (15 pts):} one specific RQ (5), falsifiable H1 + H0 (5), explicit in/out scope and matched method (5).
  \item \textbf{Lit-review coverage (20 pts):} 8--12 paper citation matrix (8), correct method/artefact columns (4), logged search strings + named venues (4), non-empty specific gap column establishing novelty (4).
  \item \textbf{Experimental rigour + reproducibility (35 pts):} variables + controls + metric defined (8), baseline run first (5), $\geq$5 reps + confound mitigation (5), script-generated figure from CSV (5), pinned environment -- stack commit + \texttt{requirements.txt} + CHECKSUMS (7), README that re-runs cold (5).
  \item \textbf{Ethics handling (20 pts):} scope-of-harm + Menlo beneficence (6), coordinated-disclosure plan with ICS-realistic timeline (7), dual-use statement with benefit-to-harm + what is withheld (7).
  \item \textbf{Writing quality (10 pts):} one-page abstract in venue structure (4), numbers-in-abstract + every claim cited (3), threats-to-validity named -- internal/external/construct (3).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Auto-fail:} any evidence the student ran the experiment against a
real plant, a third party's network, or a PLC they do not own; or a
published finding that hands a working plant attack to the public ahead
of any patch. Re-issue the lab with a written ethics warning.

\vspace{1mm}
\textbf{Bonus (up to +5):} a genuine Step-9 pre-registration committed
before the run (git log proves order) and a full threats-to-validity
subsection with a sample-size / power justification.
\end{rubricbox}
