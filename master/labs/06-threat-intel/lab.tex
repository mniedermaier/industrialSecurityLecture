\labheader

\begin{goalbox}
Take a real, recent OT-relevant ATT\&CK technique. Form a hunt hypothesis. Execute it against the Bachelor Docker lab Zeek logs. Iterate until you can either confirm or deny.
\end{goalbox}

\begin{stepbox}{1 -- Pick a TTP}
From the ATT\&CK for ICS matrix, pick one of: T0855 (Unauthorised Command Message), T0859 (Valid Accounts), T0846 (Remote System Discovery), T0867 (Lateral Tool Transfer). Note the canonical detection guidance.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{2 -- Translate to lab telemetry}
For your chosen TTP, identify what would be visible in: Zeek \texttt{conn.log}, \texttt{modbus.log}, \texttt{notice.log}, or in OpenPLC's web-UI access log.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{3 -- Generate baseline}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
cd bachelor/labs/_stack && docker compose up -d
docker compose exec student python3 /labs/scripts/modbus_poll_loop.py &
sleep 300
\end{lstlisting}
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{4 -- Inject and hunt}
Trigger your chosen TTP (e.g.\ \texttt{modbus\_inject.py}). Search the Zeek logs for the signal. Document the time-to-detection and the precision (false positives in 5 min of baseline).
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{5 -- Refine}
If your hunt was noisy, tighten the query. If it missed, broaden it. Document the iteration.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{deliverbox}
A 1-page hunt report: TTP, hypothesis, query, baseline-noise level, detection result, refinement. Bonus: a Zeek script that turns the hunt into a continuous notice.
\end{deliverbox}
