\labheader

\begin{goalbox}
Produce a complete, analyst-grade threat-intelligence product on one real OT
threat group. You will: build the group's ATT\&CK-for-ICS technique profile in
Navigator, encode a campaign as a STIX 2.1 bundle (indicators, attack-patterns,
relationships), map your existing detections back to the techniques, run a
Diamond Model analysis of one incident, and derive a consequence-driven set of
prioritised intelligence requirements (PIRs) for a named sector. The
deliverable is a 2-page threat assessment plus a machine-readable ATT\&CK layer
JSON --- the kind of artefact a SOC and a plant security lead can both act on.
\end{goalbox}

\begin{warnbox}
Most useful OT intel is \textbf{TLP-marked}. Treat anything you pull from an
ISAC, a vendor portal, or a paid feed as \textbf{TLP:AMBER} by default and do
not redistribute it. The technique IDs, public Dragos/Mandiant/CISA reports,
and the ATT\&CK matrix itself are TLP:CLEAR and safe to quote. When in doubt,
mark your own product TLP:AMBER+STRICT and keep it inside the cohort.
\end{warnbox}

\section*{Background and scope}

This lab is analysis, not exploitation. You will not touch the Bachelor Docker
stack except to confirm which detections you already own (Step 4). Everything
else is desk research, structured-data authoring, and writing. The skill being
assessed is \emph{analytic tradecraft}: can you turn a pile of public reporting
into a structured, falsifiable, decision-useful intel product?

Pick \textbf{one} threat group at Step 0 and carry it through the entire lab.
Do not switch groups halfway --- consistency across artefacts is part of the grade.

\section*{Part A -- Build the technique profile}

\begin{stepbox}{1 -- ATT\&CK-for-ICS technique profile in Navigator}
From your primary sources, extract every ATT\&CK \emph{for ICS} technique your
group has demonstrably used. Work tactic-by-tactic across the ICS matrix
(Initial Access $\to$ \dots $\to$ Impact). For each technique record the
\texttt{Txxxx} ID, the tactic, and the \emph{evidence sentence} (which report,
which incident).

In Navigator, create an ICS-domain layer, score each used technique \texttt{1},
colour it, and put your evidence sentence in the technique \texttt{comment}
field. Map the group's official \texttt{group-Gxxxx} STIX ID in the layer
metadata if it exists.

\textbf{Expected output:} a Navigator layer with 10--20 scored ICS techniques,
each carrying a one-line evidence comment, exported via
\emph{layer $\to$ download $\to$ JSON} to \texttt{group-layer.json}. Sanity
check: open the JSON and confirm \texttt{"domain": "ics-attack"} and that every
\texttt{techniqueID} starts with \texttt{T0}.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{2 -- Validate the profile against the matrix}
A common rookie error is to cite \emph{enterprise} ATT\&CK techniques
(\texttt{T1059}, \texttt{T1021}) in an ICS layer. The two matrices overlap
conceptually but use different IDs. Walk your list and for every technique
answer: is this an ICS-matrix technique (\texttt{T0xxx}) or did the activity
actually happen in the IT/enterprise domain (\texttt{T1xxx}) before pivoting to
OT?

Many real OT campaigns are \emph{dual-matrix}: enterprise techniques for the IT
intrusion, ICS techniques for the OT impact. Split your profile into an
\textbf{enterprise} sub-list and an \textbf{ICS} sub-list, and note the
pivot point (the technique where the adversary crossed from IT into OT ---
typically a \texttt{T0883} Internet-Accessible Device or \texttt{T0822}
External Remote Services hop).

\textbf{Expected output:} a two-column table (enterprise IDs / ICS IDs) and one
sentence naming the IT$\to$OT pivot technique for your group.
\end{stepbox}

\section*{Part B -- Encode the campaign as STIX 2.1}

\begin{stepbox}{3 -- Author a STIX 2.1 bundle}
Encode one campaign of your group as a STIX 2.1 bundle. At minimum include:
one \texttt{intrusion-set} (or \texttt{threat-actor}), 2--3
\texttt{attack-pattern} objects (each carrying an
\texttt{external\_reference} to its ATT\&CK ICS \texttt{Txxxx} ID), 2--3
\texttt{indicator} objects (use \emph{plausible, defanged} network or host
indicators --- do not paste live C2 you cannot verify), and the
\texttt{relationship} objects that wire them together
(\texttt{uses}, \texttt{indicates}). Add TLP marking via the standard
\texttt{marking-definition}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=python]
from stix2 import (IntrusionSet, AttackPattern, Indicator, Relationship,
                   Bundle, ExternalReference, TLP_AMBER)

grp = IntrusionSet(name="ELECTRUM",
    description="OT-capable activity group; see Dragos.",
    object_marking_refs=[TLP_AMBER])

ap = AttackPattern(name="Unauthorized Command Message",
    external_references=[ExternalReference(
        source_name="mitre-ics-attack", external_id="T0855")],
    object_marking_refs=[TLP_AMBER])

ind = Indicator(name="Suspicious Modbus write burst",
    pattern="[network-traffic:dst_port = 502]",
    pattern_type="stix",
    object_marking_refs=[TLP_AMBER])

rels = [Relationship(grp, "uses", ap),
        Relationship(ind, "indicates", grp)]

bundle = Bundle(grp, ap, ind, *rels)
open("campaign.json","w").write(bundle.serialize(pretty=True))
\end{lstlisting}

Then validate it. A bundle that does not pass the validator is not a deliverable:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
stix2_validator campaign.json
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Expected output:} \texttt{campaign.json} that the validator accepts with
no errors, containing $\geq$1 intrusion-set, $\geq$2 attack-patterns with
ATT\&CK \texttt{external\_id}s, $\geq$2 indicators, and the relationships
linking them.
\end{stepbox}

\section*{Part C -- Close the loop to detection}

\begin{stepbox}{4 -- Map detections to techniques}
Intel that does not change a detection is decoration. For each ICS technique in
your Step 1 profile, decide whether you already detect it in the Bachelor lab
telemetry, and \emph{with what}. Inventory what detections you actually have:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
cd bachelor/labs/_stack && docker compose up -d
ls zeek-logs/current/        # conn, modbus, notice logs available?
docker compose exec student ls /labs/scripts/   # injectors to test against
\end{lstlisting}

Produce a coverage table: \texttt{Technique (Txxxx)} $\to$
\texttt{Detection (Zeek notice / Sigma rule / none)} $\to$
\texttt{Telemetry source} $\to$ \texttt{Coverage (full / partial / gap)}.
Be honest about gaps --- naming a gap is worth more than overclaiming coverage.

\textbf{Expected output:} a coverage table where every Step 1 ICS technique has
a row, with at least the \texttt{T0855}/\texttt{T0836} Modbus-write techniques
mapped to a real detection and at least two honest gaps identified.
\end{stepbox}

\section*{Part D -- Structured analysis}

\begin{stepbox}{5 -- Diamond Model of one incident}
Pick \emph{one} concrete incident attributed to your group and fill all four
Diamond vertices plus the meta-features. Be specific: ``the adversary'' is not
a fill --- \emph{which} sub-cluster, \emph{which} infrastructure, \emph{which}
capability version.

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Adversary} --- the named group and any sub-cluster / operator
        tradecraft signature.
  \item \textbf{Capability} --- the specific malware/tooling and its ATT\&CK
        techniques (cross-reference your Step 1 \texttt{Txxxx} IDs).
  \item \textbf{Infrastructure} --- C2, staging, delivery (Type 1 adversary-owned
        vs.\ Type 2 hijacked).
  \item \textbf{Victim} --- sector, asset class, the \emph{consequence} the
        adversary was reaching for.
  \item \textbf{Meta:} timestamp, phase (kill-chain / ICS Cyber Kill Chain
        stage), result, confidence.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Expected output:} a completed diamond (text or diagram) with all four
vertices populated from cited sources, the capability vertex cross-referenced to
your \texttt{Txxxx} list, and an explicit confidence rating (low/med/high) with
one sentence of justification.
\end{stepbox}

\begin{stepbox}{6 -- Consequence-driven PIRs for a sector}
Write a prioritised intelligence-requirement (PIR) list for a \emph{named}
sector your group threatens (e.g.\ electric transmission, water treatment,
LNG). PIRs are questions, not statements, and they must be
\textbf{consequence-driven}: anchor each to a process consequence the operator
fears (loss of view, loss of control, equipment damage, safety trip), not to a
generic IT outcome.

Write 5--7 PIRs, ranked, each with: the question, the consequence it protects
against, the collection source that would answer it, and the trigger /
threshold that would escalate it. Example shape:
\emph{``PIR-1: Is [group] currently developing or deploying capability against
[specific protocol/controller we operate]? (Consequence: loss of control of
[process]. Source: Dragos/CISA advisories + vendor PSIRT. Trigger: any advisory
naming our controller family.)''}

\textbf{Expected output:} 5--7 ranked, consequence-anchored PIRs, each a
question with source and trigger, fitting on one page.
\end{stepbox}

\section*{Part E -- Stretch}

\begin{stepbox}{7 -- Write a detection for one TTP (stretch)}
Turn one technique from your profile into a deployable detection. Either a Sigma
rule (for an IT/log-based TTP) or a Zeek script / notice (for an OT-protocol
TTP). Tag it with the \texttt{Txxxx} ID it covers so the rule self-documents its
ATT\&CK mapping.

\begin{lstlisting}[]
title: OT Unauthorized Command - Modbus Write Burst
id: 7c0f1e2a-ot06-4b3c-9d1e-modbuswrite001
status: experimental
description: Detects a burst of Modbus write-coil/write-register
  requests from a non-EWS host (ATT&CK ICS T0855 / T0836).
logsource: { product: zeek, service: modbus }
detection:
  writes:
    func_code:
      - 5    # Write Single Coil
      - 6    # Write Single Register
      - 15   # Write Multiple Coils
      - 16   # Write Multiple Registers
  timeframe: 10s
  condition: writes | count() by id.orig_h > 20
tags: [attack.t0855, attack.t0836, attack.impair_process_control]
falsepositives: [legitimate HMI/EWS batch writes during commissioning]
level: high
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Expected output:} one syntactically valid Sigma or Zeek rule, carrying
its \texttt{Txxxx} tag, with a stated false-positive condition and a tuning note.
\end{stepbox}

\section*{Troubleshooting}

\begin{notebox}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Navigator container will not start} --- port 4200 is often busy;
        remap with \texttt{-p 4300:4200} and browse \texttt{localhost:4300}.
  \item \textbf{Navigator shows enterprise techniques only} --- you created an
        enterprise layer. Start over and choose the \emph{ICS} domain at the
        ``create new layer'' prompt.
  \item \texttt{stix2} \textbf{rejects your TLP marking} --- use the built-in
        \texttt{TLP\_AMBER} constant, not a hand-rolled marking-definition; the
        library ships the canonical UUIDs.
  \item \textbf{Validator complains about timestamps} --- let the library set
        \texttt{created}/\texttt{modified} automatically; do not pass strings.
  \item \textbf{Indicator pattern errors} --- STIX patterning is strict; wrap in
        square brackets and quote string values
        (\texttt{[file:hashes.'SHA-256' = '...']}).
  \item \textbf{No Zeek modbus.log} --- the Bachelor stack only writes it once
        Modbus traffic flows; run \texttt{modbus\_poll\_loop.py} first.
\end{itemize}
\end{notebox}

\section*{Self-check}

Before handing in, make sure you can answer each in one sentence:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item Which of your techniques are ICS-matrix (\texttt{T0xxx}) and which are
        enterprise (\texttt{T1xxx}), and where is the IT$\to$OT pivot?
  \item Does \texttt{campaign.json} pass the validator with zero errors?
  \item Which technique in your profile do you \emph{not} detect today, and what
        would it take to close that gap?
  \item Is every PIR a question anchored to a process consequence, or did one
        slip back into a generic IT outcome?
  \item What is your confidence in the Diamond attribution, and what single new
        data point would change it?
\end{itemize}
